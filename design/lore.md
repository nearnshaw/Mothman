# Mothman lore bank

Collected 2026-11-04 from public sources (see [references.md](references.md)). The owner's research sheet, read the same day, adds one pointer: the Mothman Museum's clippings archive is the primary local collection, with image rights to be checked before any reuse. Everything here is "as reported", and the reports disagree with each other, which is itself useful material.

## Timeline

- **12 Nov 1966, Clendenin WV.** Five gravediggers see a "brown human being" lift out of the trees and fly over them.
- **15 Nov 1966, the TNT area, Point Pleasant.** Two young couples, Roger and Linda Scarberry and Steve and Mary Mallette, driving the old munitions plant roads around midnight, see two large red eyes by an old generator building and a winged figure 6–7 ft tall. It follows their car toward town at speeds they put at 100 mph, up to the city limits. They report to Deputy Sheriff Millard Halstead. On the road they had seen a dead dog; when they come back it is gone. Linda could not make out a face because of the eyes. Steve: "It was like a man with wings." Mary Mallette is the only witness to report ear trouble afterward.
- **15 Nov 1966, ~10:30 pm, Salem WV.** Newell Partridge's television goes dark and gives a rising whine. His hunting dog Bandit howls, chases two glowing red circles toward the hay barn and is never seen again. Partridge says the eyes glowed "like bicycle reflectors" when he aimed his flashlight at them.
- **16 Nov 1966.** The *Point Pleasant Register* prints the story. The press names the creature after the *Batman* TV show (the villain Killer Moth existed in the comics). Two volunteer firemen report a "large bird with red eyes". Sheriff George Johnson suggests an unusually large heron.
- **16 Nov 1966.** Marcella Bennett, visiting the Ralph Thomas family near the TNT area, sees a grey figure rise beside her car; she drops her baby daughter and runs. The family locks the doors while it peers in through the windows. It is gone before police arrive. Bennett later needs medical help for nightmares.
- **27 Nov 1966.** Connie Carpenter reports a daytime sighting with red eyes (relayed by Keel; a sceptic later notes the only red-eye report he could trace was second-hand).
- **Dec 1966.** John Keel arrives and starts collecting reports: TVs, phones and police radios failing since autumn, cars stalling, poltergeist cases. The James Lilley family sells their house.
- **Jan 1967.** Mary Hyre, Point Pleasant correspondent for the *Athens Messenger*, receives over 500 calls after one weekend of sightings. A short man with thick glasses and a bowl haircut visits her office, asks for directions, stares at her, picks up a pen as if he has never seen one, laughs and runs. Weeks later he is seen hurrying into a large black car.
- **Through 1967.** Keel counts at least 100 witnesses; the state encyclopedia counts 26 sightings in a year. UFO lights are reported over the river and the plant.
- **15 Dec 1967, ~5 pm.** The Silver Bridge between Point Pleasant and Kanauga, Ohio, collapses under rush-hour and Christmas-shopping traffic. 46 people die; two are never found. The 1971 investigation blames stress-corrosion cracking in a single eyebar of the suspension chain. That evening the Lilley family counts more than twelve lights flashing over their house before they vanish into the forest.
- **Christmas week 1967.** A man in a black suit visits Hyre asking about UFOs, not the bridge; the same night a matching man calls on several witnesses claiming to be a reporter from Cambridge, Ohio, but cannot say where Columbus is.
- **After.** Sightings fade. A later legend ties the creature to the bridge: Mothman as omen. There were no reports in the immediate aftermath of the collapse.
- **1970.** Mary Hyre dies in Point Pleasant after a four-week illness.
- **1970 / 1975.** Gray Barker's heavily fictionalised *The Silver Bridge*; Keel's *The Mothman Prophecies*.
- **2002.** Film with Richard Gere; the Mothman Festival starts (third weekend of September, 10–12k visitors, pancake-eating contest, hayrides). **2003:** Bob Roach's 12 ft steel statue downtown. **2006:** the Mothman Museum opens.
- **2011–2020.** A Mothman-like figure reported repeatedly around Chicago (55 reports compiled by 2017; a self-selected sample).
- **2016.** A photo on Route 2, Mason County, probably an owl carrying a frog or snake.

## The creature, as described

- Height 5–7 ft (Linda Scarberry: about 7 ft, "a slender, muscular man" with wings). Wider than a man.
- Wings bat-like, ~10 ft span (Roger Scarberry). It **glided rather than flapped** and could **rise straight up "like a helicopter"**.
- Body grey or brown; in some tellings black with white wings; reddish flesh around the eyes (the crane-shaped version).
- Eyes large, glowing red, set near the top of the shoulders, "hypnotic"; no one could describe a face.
- Sound: a **hum** in flight; a **screech**; Bennett: like "a woman screaming".
- Reported effects nearby: TV and radio static, a rising whine, phones dead, cars stalling, dogs gone.

## The place

- The **TNT area**: a WWII munitions plant north of Point Pleasant, several hundred acres of woods, concrete storage domes ("igloos") for explosives, a network of tunnels, an old power/generator building, fences. Next to it the **McClintic Wildlife Management Area**: dense forest, ponds, steep ridges, dirt roads used as lovers' lanes.
- The **Silver Bridge** carried US 35 over the Ohio River to Gallipolis / Kanauga. 44 of the dead are buried in Gallipolis.
- Point Pleasant itself: river town at the mouth of the Kanawha; site of the 1774 Battle of Point Pleasant.

## Explanations offered

- **Sandhill crane** off its migration route (Robert L. Smith, WVU): human height, 7 ft span, red skin around the eyes. Witnesses rejected it.
- **Great blue heron** (statistically likelier where eyeshine was not mentioned).
- **Barred owl eyeshine** (Joe Nickell), plus documented hoaxes: workers tying flashlights to helium balloons.
- **Snowy owl**: Ace Henry shot one with a ~5 ft span in 1966; the local paper called it a "giant owl". It is displayed at the museum.
- **California condor**: a 1966 letter to the *Huntington Herald Dispatch* by a teenage Breece D'J Pancake.
- **Folklore drift** (Jan Harold Brunvand): motifs shared with older tales, including the creature attacking parked cars with teenagers inside.
- **The Cornstalk curse**: the Shawnee leader Cornstalk, murdered at the fort in 1777, is said in local lore to have cursed the place for 200 years. Linked after the fact to Mothman and the bridge.
- **Keel's view**: not extraterrestrials; "ultraterrestrials", old things that appear as UFOs, monsters, demons or ghosts, and Point Pleasant as a "window" area of prolonged strangeness. He also thought some sightings were owls seen by people already frightened. Keel's own letters from the time differ from the book (Sherwood, *Skeptical Inquirer* 2002).

## Adjacent figures

- **Indrid Cold.** On 2 November 1966 salesman Woodrow Derenberger says a man stepped from a craft on Route 77 near Parkersburg, grinned, and told him: "Be not frightened. We mean you no harm." He later claimed visits to Cold's planet, Lanulos. Keel links Cold to the flap; others treat them as separate.
- **Men in Black.** Keel and Hyre's accounts: odd, out-of-place men asking about UFOs, not the creature.
- **Mary Hyre.** The reporter who took the town seriously; Keel's friend and co-investigator.
